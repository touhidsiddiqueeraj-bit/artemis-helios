# EDITOR-IN-CHIEF COMMENTS:

1. INTRODUCTION section
         To strengthen the quality and clarity of your manuscript, we
recommend that the Introduction section be organized systematically and
include the following elements:
a). Context – Provide the background and general setting of the research
topic to help readers understand the relevance.
b) Highlight the Problems – Clearly state the specific issues or
challenges that motivate the study.
c) State of the Art – Summarize the most recent and relevant literature to
show the current progress in the field.
d) Gap Analysis – Identify what is missing or insufficient in existing
studies, and explain why further research is needed.
e) Contribution – Describe the novelty and main contributions of your work
compared to previous studies.
f) Significance – Emphasize the importance and potential impact of your
findings for the research community and practical applications.
By structuring the Introduction in this way, your paper will present a clear
rationale, demonstrate originality, and highlight its relevance to both
academic and practical audiences.

2. CONCLUSION section
         To ensure that the Conclusion provides a clear and impactful
closure, please structure it with the following elements:
a). What Has Been Done
        Explicitly state the main actions and achievements of your study
(e.g., “In this work, we developed…, implemented…, and
validated…”).
        This should directly connect to the objectives and gap identified in
the Introduction section.
b). Summary of Key Findings
        Highlight the most important results and how they address the
research problem.
        Emphasize the novelty and contribution compared to previous studies.
c). Limitations
        Briefly acknowledge any constraints or weaknesses that may affect
generalizability.
        Keep this concise and factual.
d). Future Directions
        Suggest possible improvements, extensions, or new research questions
arising from your study.
        Indicate how future work can build upon your contributions.

experimental procedure that includes all of the information needed to

         To ensure clarity, coherence, and strong scientific impact, please

a) Presentation of Results

Reviewer A:

The paper addresses MPPT improvement for photovoltaic Solar Home Systems
operating under highly variable monsoon conditions in Bangladesh. A key
strength of the work is its relevance to renewable energy and photovoltaic
control, which fits well within the scope of IJPEDS. The manuscript is also
generally well organized, following a logical flow from system design to
simulation results and discussion. The language is generally understandable
and readable, although several sentences are excessively long and dense, and
professional English proofreading would improve clarity and readability.

The main limitation is the disconnect between the practical deployment
claims and the evidence provided. Although the controller is presented as a
solution for real Solar Home System deployment, Section V.C explicitly
states that the study is entirely simulation-based and that no hardware
measurements or field validation have been performed. As a result, the large
performance gains reported in Table III remain unverified in practice. The
validation presented in Section IV.E only compares simulated baseline P&O
results with literature data and does not validate the proposed controller
itself. In addition, conclusions regarding benefits for the IDCOL SHS
program, large-scale deployment impact, and economic advantages extend
beyond what can be demonstrated from synthetic simulations alone.

The reference list is relatively limited, containing only 13 references.
While the current references include both foundational MPPT literature and
some recent publications, the overall coverage is not sufficient to
establish a comprehensive state-of-the-art review. However, additional
recent references from leading journals in photovoltaic systems, MPPT
control, embedded AI, and power electronics would strengthen the novelty
positioning.

There are also formatting and presentation issues that should be addressed.
The citation numbering should be carefully checked to ensure that references
appear in proper sequential order throughout the manuscript. The manuscript
should also be revised to fully comply with the latest IJPEDS template and
formatting requirements, particularly for standard sections such as Funding
Information, Author Contributions Statement, Conflict of Interest Statement,
and Data Availability Statement.

The paper is currently written by a single author. Although single-author
papers are permitted in international journals and do not constitute a
scientific weakness by themselves, studies involving controller design,
machine learning development, simulation modeling, hardware architecture,
and deployment analysis typically benefit from collaboration and independent
technical verification. The manuscript would be strengthened if the
contributions of each research activity were transparently documented
through a formal Author Contributions Statement following international
publishing standards.

The topic is relevant and the proposed approach shows promise, but the
current evidence supports only simulation-level conclusions. Hardware
validation, stronger verification of the irradiance model, a more
comprehensive literature foundation, improved compliance with journal
formatting requirements, and more cautious interpretation of practical
deployment benefits are needed before the claims can be considered
sufficiently supported.

Reviewer B:

The paper addresses a relevant topic for IJPEDS by combining LSTM-based
irradiance prediction with MPPT control for photovoltaic systems. The
proposed dual-MCU architecture is interesting and the reported efficiency
gains under monsoon conditions could be valuable for off-grid solar
applications.

However, several important issues limit the current contribution. The most
significant concern is that all results are simulation-based. Section V.C
explicitly states that no hardware measurements were performed, yet the main
claims rely on the efficiency improvements reported in Table III and Figures
5–8. Without experimental validation, it is difficult to assess the
practical effectiveness of the controller. In addition, Sections III.C and
III.D do not provide sufficient details regarding LSTM training, synthetic
dataset generation, parameter selection, and Monte Carlo procedures, which
limits reproducibility. The Introduction and Literature Review also do not
clearly establish how the proposed dual-MCU architecture advances beyond
existing LSTM-based predictive MPPT approaches.

The manuscript also does not appear to follow the standard journal
manuscript template. Several formatting elements commonly required by IJPEDS
are missing or incomplete. The reference list contains only 13 references,
which is relatively limited for a research article covering MPPT, machine
learning, photovoltaic systems, and embedded control. Additional recent and
relevant references, particularly from the last five years, should be
included to better support the novelty claims and contextualize the work
within current research trends. Furthermore, an author biography section is
not provided and should be added if required by the journal format.

These issues appear fixable through stronger validation, clearer
methodological reporting, improved manuscript formatting, expansion of the
literature review and reference base, and a more rigorous novelty analysis
relative to recent studies.

The topic is within the scope of IJPEDS and has reasonable publication
potential. However, the absence of experimental validation, insufficient
methodological transparency, limited literature coverage, and non-compliance
with several manuscript preparation requirements currently prevent reliable
verification and evaluation of the reported contributions.
